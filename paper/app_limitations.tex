\section{Limitations}
\label{app:limitations}

WingBench focuses on transonic wing design under a fixed simulation protocol
and computational budget. Ten seeds per method and task provide limited power
to detect small performance differences. Delivery rates also depend on the
computing environment: time limits account for 31\% of baseline nondeliveries,
with their impact bounded in Appendix~\ref{app:outcomes}. Planning results
capture the combined contributions of the selected plans and the executor's
reasoning. Future work could extend the benchmark to other design domains,
increase the number of independent runs, and evaluate additional search
strategies across different computational budgets. The present study does not
include an adjoint comparison and does not establish competitiveness with
aerodynamic optimization practice.

The evaluator records success under a particular mesh recipe, solver and
execution limits. Upstream consistency and source-geometry checks do not
establish mesh independence or universal feasibility. The layer sensitivity
in Appendix~\ref{app:evaluator-checks} shows that some recorded mesh failures
are recoverable under different numerical settings. Agent transition counts
and selected cases motivate hypotheses, but do not isolate prompt effects;
bounded recoveries and executable-plan substitutions also affect the tested
implementations (Appendix~\ref{app:agent-behavior}).

The cross-family comparison uses different prior CFD information and surrogate
access. Equal online query counts do not imply equal resources: surrogate
usage differs at intermediate checkpoints, and model calls and elapsed session
time differ among the agents. Optimizer CPU/GPU time, model-server compute and
consistent end-to-end wall time were not recorded across all methods
(Appendix~\ref{app:resources}). The open-ended extension was selected after the
four-query comparison and its best-found design need not be its recommendation.
The records therefore support performance under the stated configurations,
not a ranking under fully matched information and compute budgets.
